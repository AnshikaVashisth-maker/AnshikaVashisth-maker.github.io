Treatment Options for ADHD

ADHD can be effectively managed with medication, behavioral strategies, therapy, or a combination of approaches. The best treatment depends on your individual needs, preferences, and how your brain responds.

Medication for ADHD

Stimulant Medications

Stimulants work by increasing dopamine and norepinephrine in the brain, improving focus and impulse control. They are the most commonly prescribed ADHD medications.

Methylphenidate-Based (Ritalin, Concerta, Daytrana)

How it works: Blocks the reabsorption of dopamine and norepinephrine

Duration: 4-12 hours depending on formulation

Effectiveness: Works for 70-80% of people

Common side effects: Decreased appetite, insomnia, increased heart rate, headaches

Best for: People who need all-day focus; good for school/work settings

Amphetamine-Based (Adderall, Vyvanse, Dexedrine)

How it works: Stimulates release of dopamine and norepinephrine

Duration: 4-14 hours depending on formulation

Effectiveness: Works for 70-80% of people

Common side effects: Similar to methylphenidate; may be more potent

Best for: People needing stronger effects; longer duration needed

Non-Stimulant Medications

Non-stimulants work differently and may be better for some people, especially those with anxiety, substance abuse history, or side effect concerns.

Atomoxetine (Strattera)

How it works: Increases norepinephrine by blocking its reabsorption

Duration: 24 hours

Effectiveness: Works for 60-70% of people

Common side effects: Nausea, fatigue, mood changes

Best for: People who don't tolerate stimulants; those with anxiety; athletes concerned about appetite

Guanfacine (Intuniv) & Clonidine (Kapvay)

How it works: Alpha-adrenergic agonists; help regulate norepinephrine

Duration: 24 hours

Effectiveness: Moderate; often combined with other medications

Common side effects: Drowsiness, low blood pressure, headaches

Best for: People with hyperactivity/impulsivity; those with tics or Tourette's syndrome

Viloxazine (Qelbree)

How it works: Selective norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor

Duration: 24 hours

Effectiveness: Newer medication; 60-70% effectiveness

Common side effects: Nausea, insomnia, appetite changes

Best for: People seeking non-stimulant option; longer-acting medication

Other Medications

Antidepressants: Sometimes used off-label (bupropion, fluoxetine) if stimulants don't work or comorbid depression/anxiety exists

Blood pressure medications: Guanfacine and clonidine were originally developed for hypertension but help with ADHD

Behavioral Strategies

Non-medication approaches that help manage ADHD symptoms daily:

Organization & Time Management

- Use calendars and planners (digital or paper)

- Break large tasks into smaller steps

- Use timers and alarms for time awareness

- Create consistent routines and habits

- Use checklists for daily tasks

Environmental Modifications

- Minimize distractions (quiet workspace, noise-canceling headphones)

- Organize your physical space

- Use visual cues and reminders

- Establish a dedicated study/work area

Executive Function Support

- External accountability (buddy system, check-ins)

- Using apps for task management (Todoist, Notion, etc.)

- Body doubling (working near someone else)

- Reward systems for completed tasks

Physical Activity

- Regular exercise (30+ minutes, 3-5 times per week)

- Sports or movement-based activities

- Yoga or meditation

- Physical activity regulates dopamine naturally

Sleep, Nutrition & Lifestyle

- Consistent sleep schedule (8-10 hours for teens)

- Balanced diet with protein and whole grains

- Limit sugar and caffeine (can worsen symptoms)

- Reduce screen time before bed

Therapy & Coaching

Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT)

Helps you identify thought patterns that worsen ADHD and develop coping strategies. Especially helpful for anxiety and mood issues alongside ADHD.

ADHD Coaching

A coach works with you to develop systems for time management, organization, and goal-setting. Different from therapy—focused on practical skills.

Family Therapy

Helps family members understand ADHD and develop strategies to support you. Reduces conflict and improves communication.

School/Workplace Accommodations

- Extended test time

- Reduced distraction testing environment

- Assignment deadlines with check-ins

- Preferential seating

- Note-taking support

Combination Treatment

Most effective approach combines:

- Medication (if chosen) + Behavioral strategies + Therapy/coaching

This addresses ADHD from multiple angles and increases success rates significantly.

Finding the Right Treatment for You

Treatment is highly individual. What works for one person may not work for another. Work with your doctor to:

- Start with one approach (usually medication or therapy)

- Track how you're responding (mood, focus, side effects)

- Adjust dosages or approaches as needed

- Give each treatment 4-6 weeks before evaluating

- Be patient—finding the right treatment takes time

Common Questions About ADHD Treatment

Will I be on medication forever?

Not necessarily. Some people take medication long-term; others use it short-term while building coping skills. Discuss with your doctor what's right for you.

Are stimulants addictive?

When prescribed and taken as directed for ADHD, stimulants are not addictive. However, discuss this concern with your doctor.

Can medication change my personality?

Good medication management allows you to be your true self by managing symptoms. Some people feel "more like themselves" on proper treatment.

What if medication doesn't work?

Different medications work for different people. Your doctor can try different medications, dosages, or switch to non-stimulants. Behavioral strategies also help.
