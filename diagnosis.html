Getting Diagnosed with ADHD

Signs You Might Have ADHD

If you're experiencing several of these symptoms consistently, it may be worth talking to a doctor:

Attention & Focus Issues

- Difficulty sustaining attention in conversations or tasks

- Getting distracted easily by irrelevant things

- Trouble organizing thoughts or activities

- Frequently losing important items (keys, phone, homework)

- Procrastinating on important tasks

Hyperactivity & Impulsivity

- Feeling restless or fidgety most of the time

- Difficulty staying seated when expected

- Talking excessively or interrupting others

- Acting without thinking about consequences

- Difficulty waiting your turn

Emotional & Social Challenges

- Difficulty managing emotions or mood swings

- Low self-esteem or feeling "broken"

- Struggling to maintain relationships

- Time management difficulties

The Diagnostic Process

Step 1: Initial Consultation

Schedule an appointment with a healthcare provider—usually a psychiatrist, psychologist, or neurologist. During this first visit, they'll:

- Ask about your symptoms and when they started

- Discuss how symptoms affect school, work, and relationships

- Take a family history (ADHD often runs in families)

- Rule out other conditions with similar symptoms

Step 2: Questionnaires & Rating Scales

You'll fill out standardized questionnaires such as:

- ADHD Rating Scale (ADHD-RS)

- Conners Rating Scale

- Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale (ASRS)

These help doctors assess symptom severity and type.

Step 3: Psychological Testing

Some providers use computerized tests or performance-based assessments to evaluate:

- Attention and focus

- Processing speed

- Working memory

- Impulse control

Step 4: Medical Evaluation

Your doctor may order:

- Blood tests (to rule out medical causes)

- Thyroid function tests

- Sometimes brain imaging (EEG or MRI)

Step 5: Feedback & Diagnosis

After all assessments, your doctor will discuss:

- Whether you have ADHD and what type

- Your specific symptom profile

- Treatment options available

- Next steps for management

How to Talk to a Doctor

If you suspect you have ADHD but haven't been diagnosed:

Before Your Appointment

- Write down your symptoms and when they occur

- Note how ADHD affects your daily life (school, work, relationships)

- Gather information about family members with ADHD

- Write any questions you want to ask

During Your Appointment

- Be honest about your struggles

- Describe specific examples of how symptoms affect you

- Mention if other family members have ADHD

- Ask about different types of ADHD

- Ask what tests they recommend

Questions to Ask Your Doctor

- Do I have ADHD? What type?

- What treatment options are available?

- What are the pros and cons of medication?

- What therapy or coaching options exist?

- How will we monitor my treatment?

- Are there school/work accommodations I can get?

Different Types of Providers

Psychiatrist: Medical doctor who can diagnose ADHD and prescribe medication

Psychologist: Can diagnose ADHD and provide therapy; cannot prescribe medication (in most states)

Neuropsychologist: Specializes in comprehensive testing of brain function

Pediatrician/Primary Care Doctor: Can diagnose ADHD, especially in children

After Diagnosis

Once diagnosed, you can:

- Start treatment (medication, therapy, or both)

- Request accommodations at school or work

- Join support communities

- Understand yourself better

- Develop strategies that work for YOUR brain
